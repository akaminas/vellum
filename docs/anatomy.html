Signature

  1. 1White page, cream cards

    White surface (charcoal #2A2520 in the dark twin), warm greys. Panels and tiles are cream; figures sit on white, so one version suits a journal.

  2. 2Claim title

    A sentence with a verb, in Palatino, left aligned, in ink. All other text is a humanist sans.

  3. 3Six families, fixed order

    Series use tones in the order teal, terracotta, indigo, ochre, mauve, moss. Areas use tints; emphasis and text on a tint use the deep step.

  4. 4Direct labels

    Lines end in a ringed dot and an ink label. Up to four series: no legend. Five or six: legend plus end labels. Seven or more: fold or facet.

  5. 5Hairline grid, no frame

    Solid horizontal hairlines, one step off the surface. No top, left or right spine, no box, no vertical or dashed grid.

  6. 6Unit line

    Quantity and unit in muted text above the y axis, upright, instead of a rotated label. The x label stays under its axis.

  7. 7Traceable footer

    Bottom left, small, muted: data source, script and git commit (with -dirty for an unclean tree).

Typefaces

Title face

Palatino

A claim in Palatino reads at arm’s length and from two metres away

Old-style serif with open counters, legible at 13 pt on a page and on a poster at two metres. Used only for the claim title and slide or poster headings.

Windows: Palatino Linotype · LaTeX: TeX Gyre Pagella · web: 'Palatino Linotype', 'TeX Gyre Pagella', Palatino, serif

Body face

Corbel

parts per million · 1958 · 2020 · γ = 0.5 · −3 °C · 12,045 deaths

Humanist sans with old-style numerals, Greek, subscript digits and a true minus (which Gill Sans lacks). Used for labels, ticks, unit line, annotations, legends, footer and report body text.

Windows: Corbel · LaTeX and the web: Lato · fallback: Segoe UI, then DejaVu Sans

Weights and italics. One weight per face. Italics only for maths variables (VA).

Tables use lining figures. font-variant-numeric: lining-nums tabular-nums on the web, \usepackage[lining]{lato} in LaTeX.

Type scale

Four levels: claim, body, secondary (unit, annotation, ticks) and footer. Sizes less than 1.5 pt apart are merged.

Figures are drawn at print size and not rescaled. Slides multiply by 2.2, posters by 2.6.

Text is ink or muted, never a series colour; a label inside a tint uses the family's deep step.

Writing

  1. Sentence case. No full stop after a title or label; a note that is a sentence ends with one.
  2. The title is a claim with a verb.
  3. The unit line is a noun phrase: "parts per million", "share of GDP, per cent".
  4. True minus (U+2212). Units after a space: 10 km, 5 %.
  5. Greek as letters (γ, α), with thin spaces around "=".
  6. No project-internal abbreviations: "generations", not "gens".
  7. Footer: "NOAA GML, 1958–2020 · co2_keeling.py · git 99753b9".

Specimen

A figure with its eleven parts numbered.

Title block

Flush with the left edge of the plot area. The subtitle gives data set, sample and key settings. In a journal the claim moves to the caption and the block is dropped.

Panel title and unit line

Each panel: a bold letter and a 9 pt claim in ink, then the unit line above the y axis. The x label stays under the axis, in muted.

Plot area

No tick marks, only 8 pt muted labels: three to five per axis, at round numbers.

Footer

In a journal its content moves to the caption or the data statement.

Marks

The marker ring keeps it legible where it crosses a line. Bars have no stroke; the gap separates them.

In a paper, lines drop to 1.2 pt, member lines to 0.6 pt and markers to 20 pt² with a 1.0 pt ring.

Spacers

Gaps between fills

Stacked segments, stacked areas and heat-map cells: 1.2 pt of surface colour. Adjacent bars: 0.4 of the slot. No outlines.

Rings around dots

A surface-colour ring keeps a dot legible over a line or another dot.

Labels

  1. Line labels: at the end, ink, 7.5 pt, 6 pt right of the end dot. Colliding labels are spread in order with a 0.5 pt muted leader (endlabels in vellum.py, endLabels in vellum-web.js; same spread algorithm).
  2. Callouts: ink text, thin muted leader, no arrowhead.
  3. Reference lines: labelled at the right end, muted, above the line.
  4. Quiet notes (band meaning, seed count): muted, 7.5 pt, inside the plot away from the data.
  5. Values on marks only when the value is the point, e.g. a short bar chart.
  6. Legend only for five or six series: top left inside the plot, no frame, end labels kept.

Named sizes

V.size("double") returns the size in inches; the other tools share the table.

Thesis at 125 mm text width: 4.92 in. Journals use their own widths where they differ (88 or 180 mm for Nature). Infographics use dpi=100, so 1080 px is 10.8 in.

Multi-panel figures

Bold letters a, b, c before each panel claim. Panels in reading order, the finding last (bottom right). Shared axes share ticks; one unit line per row. With per-panel title blocks, gaps are 0.42 of a panel width and 0.55 of a panel height.

Small multiples: same axes, one family per panel, other members in rule grey behind.
