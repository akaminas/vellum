Type and anatomy

Every figure is built from the same parts, in the same places

A reader who has seen one Vellum figure knows where to look in the next: the claim at the top left, the unit above the axis, the labels at the ends of the lines, the source at the bottom. This page specifies those parts, their sizes, and the two typefaces that set them.

Two faces, one for the claim and one for everything else

Title face

Palatino

A claim in Palatino reads at arm’s length and from two metres away

An old-style serif with open counters that stays legible at 9 pt on a poster seen from two metres and at 13 pt on a page. It carries only the figure title, which is the claim, and slide or poster headings. Never axis text or labels.

Windows: Palatino Linotype · LaTeX: TeX Gyre Pagella · web: 'Palatino Linotype', 'TeX Gyre Pagella', Palatino, serif

Body face

Corbel

parts per million · 1958 · 2020 · γ = 0.5 · −3 °C · 12,045 deaths

A humanist sans with old-style numerals, so a tick label reads like text rather than like a counter. It has Greek, subscript digits and a true minus sign, which Gill Sans lacks. It carries labels, ticks, the unit line, annotations, legends, the footer and the body of a report.

Windows: Corbel · LaTeX and the web: Lato · fallback: Segoe UI, then DejaVu Sans

Restraint is the other half. One weight of each face. No italics in a figure, except for variable names in mathematics. Words are upright; a variable is italic maths (VA).

Old-style numerals do not align in a column. In a table of numbers switch to lining figures: font-variant-numeric: lining-nums tabular-nums on the web, \usepackage[lining]{lato} in LaTeX.

Four levels of text, not eight

The scale has four levels: the claim, the body, the secondary (unit, annotation, ticks) and the footer. Two sizes that differ by less than 1.5 pt should be the same size.

A figure is drawn at the size it will be printed and never scaled afterwards, so these are the sizes on the page. On a slide, multiply by 2.2; on a poster, by 2.6. The poster is the only medium where a figure is scaled, and only by that fixed factor, so the whole scale moves together.

Text never wears a series colour. Titles, labels and values are in ink; secondary text is muted. Identity comes from the mark beside the text. The one exception is a label set inside a tint, which takes the family's deep step.

Writing inside a figure

  1. Sentence case everywhere. No full stop at the end of a title or a label. A note that is a sentence ends with a stop.
  2. The title is a claim with a verb.
  3. The unit line is a noun phrase: "parts per million", "years of life expected at birth", "share of GDP, per cent".
  4. A true minus sign (U+2212), not a hyphen. Units after a space: 10 km, 5 %.
  5. Greek letters as letters (γ, α), not spelled out, with thin spaces around "=" in labels.
  6. No abbreviation a reader outside the project would not know. "Generations", not "gens".
  7. The footer names the source, the script and the stamp: "NOAA GML, 1958–2020 · co2_keeling.py · git 99753b9".

The three-second test. Cover the title. If a colleague cannot say in three seconds what the figure claims, the figure has too much in it. Remove a series or split a panel.

The parts, top to bottom

The specimen below is a real Vellum figure with its parts numbered. The key beside it gives each part's rule.

Title block

Top left, flush with the left edge of the plot area. Line one: the claim, 13 pt Palatino, ink. Line two: the subtitle, 9 pt, muted: the data set, the sample, the key settings. The subtitle sits one title height below the title, whatever the height of the figure. In a journal the claim moves to the caption and the title block is dropped.

Panel title and unit line

A multi-panel figure gives each panel a bold letter and a claim at 9 pt in ink. Under it, the unit line at 7.5 pt in muted, left aligned above the y axis: the quantity and its unit. There is no rotated y label. The x label stays under the axis, in muted.

Plot area

No frame. The bottom spine in the grid colour at 0.8 pt; no left, top or right spine. Horizontal gridlines only, hairline at 0.6 pt, solid, in the grid colour, behind the data. No tick marks, only labels, in muted at 8 pt. Three to five ticks per axis, at round numbers.

Footer

Bottom left, 6.5 pt, muted: the source, the script that drew it, and the git stamp with -dirty when the tree was not clean. A figure without a footer cannot be traced and is not finished. In a journal the footer's content moves to the caption or the data statement.

Marks

Thin marks and a quiet grid make the data the loudest thing on the page. The ring on a marker keeps it legible where it crosses a line. A stroke around a bar is never used: the gap between bars does the separating.

In a paper the page is denser than a slide, so lines drop to 1.2 pt, member lines to 0.6 pt and markers to 20 pt² with a 1.0 pt ring.

The two spacers

A gap of surface colour separates touching fills

The segments of a stacked bar, the layers of a stacked area and the cells of a heat map (1.2 pt), and adjacent bars (the 0.4 of the slot left empty). Nothing is outlined.

A ring of surface colour separates overlapping dots

The ring is what keeps a dot legible where it crosses a line or another dot. It is part of the marker, not an outline.

Direct labels first

  1. A line is labelled at its end, in ink, 7.5 pt, six points to the right of the end dot. When ends collide, labels are pushed apart in order and joined to their line by a 0.5 pt leader in muted. vellum.py does this with endlabels; vellum-web.js with endLabels; both use the same spread algorithm.
  2. A point of interest is labelled with a callout: ink text, a thin muted leader, no arrowhead.
  3. A reference line is labelled at its right end, muted, above the line.
  4. A quiet note (what the band is, how many seeds) goes in muted at 7.5 pt inside the plot, where the data are not.
  5. Numbers on marks only when the number is the point: values on a short bar chart, never a value on every point of a line.
  6. A legend box only for five or six series, flush top left inside the plot, without a frame; the end labels stay.

Named sizes

A figure is drawn at the size it will be printed. V.size("double") in Python returns the size in inches; the other tools use the same table.

A thesis at 125 mm text width draws at 4.92 in wide. A paper uses the journal's own widths when they differ (88 or 180 mm for the Nature family). An infographic is drawn at its pixel size with dpi=100, so a 1080 px square is a 10.8 in figure.

Multi-panel figures

Letters a, b, c in bold before the panel claim. Panels in reading order, with the finding last: the eye leaves a page at the bottom right, so that is where the result goes. Shared axes share ticks, and the unit line appears once per row. Gaps between panels are 0.42 of a panel width horizontally and 0.55 of a panel height vertically when each panel has a title block.

Small multiples take the same axes everywhere, one family per panel, and the other members in rule grey behind, so each panel shows its member against the whole.
