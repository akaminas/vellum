Recipe

  1. Write the claim in one sentence. It becomes the title. If it needs two sentences, make two figures.
  2. Pick the form by the job of the data: change over time, comparison, distribution, relation, part of a whole, map, tree or network. Chart forms.
  3. Pick the medium and take its sizes: width, type sizes, and whether the dark twin applies. Media.
  4. Load the palette through the tool's module and assign tones in the fixed order. Never past six: fold the rest into "other" or facet.
  5. Build the anatomy: claim title, unit line, end labels, ringed dots, hairline grid, footer with source and git stamp. Anatomy.
  6. Run the checklist, then render the figure and look at it; the validator checks colour, not collisions. Checklist.
  7. Save PDF for print and PNG at 300 dpi for screens, with the git stamp in the footer or the file name.

One number goes in the text. "Life expectancy at birth was 73.2 years in 2023, against 46.4 in 1950" belongs in a sentence or a stat tile. So does a two-slice pie. Use a table when the reader will look values up.

Removal test

Remove the element, or replace it with one flat colour. If the figure loses information, the element was data; if not, delete it.

The test removes gradients that encode nothing, 3D on flat data, shadows, bevels, outlines on bars and dots, plot boxes, and legends that repeat the end labels. It keeps a heat map's colour scale, a third axis that is the shape of a surface, and a hatch that marks a missing value.

  • a gradient as a page, card or slide background
  • a 3D effect on 2D data
  • a glow, shadow, bevel or reflection
  • an outline around a bar or a dot
  • a box around the plot
  • a legend box for four series or fewer

Defaults

  • The page is white; cream is used only for cards. A figure on white needs no second version for a journal.
  • Plain forms by default. At most one unusual form per piece, with a sentence on how to read it.
  • A circular form only when the angle means something: a season, an hour, a compass bearing.
  • Pastel tints for areas, muted tones for lines and dots. A pastel line on white is too faint to read.
  • Tint, tone and shade are computed per hue in OKLCH, and their proportions are fixed.
  • Annotations sit beside the data; the legend is secondary or absent.
  • Figures are regenerated from data by code, and a set of figures shares one style file.

Never

  • a dashed gridline
  • a rainbow colour scale
  • a pie with more than six slices
  • a title that is a noun phrase
  • text in a series colour (the mark beside the text gives identity)
  • a seventh series colour (fold, facet or grey out instead)
  • 3D axes, except for a surface or a 3D scatter with a 2D twin

Scope

Thesis figures, TikZ diagrams, modelling work, reports, policy briefs, posters, slides, pitch decks, infographics and notebooks. The medium sets sizes and density; the palette, fonts and anatomy stay the same, so figures made months apart in different tools read as one set.
