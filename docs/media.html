Papers · 89 or 183 mm · matplotlib

Paper figure

The title block is removed. The claim becomes the first sentence of the caption and the panel claims its lettered sentences; sources go in the data and code availability statement, and the file name carries the stamp.

Drawn at final width: 3.5 in (89 mm) single column, 7.2 in (183 mm) double. Lines 1.2 pt, markers 20 pt², at most four series per panel. The tones alternate in lightness, so the figure survives greyscale printing. A journal's own requirements, such as a named font, take precedence.

Files for a journal

One vector PDF per figure with fonts embedded (pdf.fonttype 42), named fig2_claim_<sha>.pdf. TIFF or EPS converted from the PDF at 600 dpi. Composite figures are assembled in the plotting script so they regenerate. At most one 3D figure, with its 2D twin as panel b.

Dissertation · 125 mm text width

Dissertation

Each figure keeps its claim title and subtitle so it can be read outside the chapter; the caption restates the claim and explains how to read the figure.

Body in TeX Gyre Pagella 11 pt on a 125 mm text width; Lato for captions, tables, margin notes and figure text. Figures drawn 4.92 in wide. Chapter numbers in teal, links in indigo without boxes. Tables in Lato with lining figures, no vertical rules, 0.5 pt rules at the header and foot, and alternate rows in the card colour.

Colours across chapters. Model in teal, data and highlights in terracotta, bounds in dotted ochre, and one gradient per variable throughout the thesis.

One chapter is printed in greyscale before the final build; a figure that fails gets a label.

Conference poster · A0 portrait · beamerposter

Conference poster

One claim, read from two metres. White sheet, cream column cards, one central figure, three short text columns and a 90 pt Palatino headline. Body 28 pt, figure text 23 pt, nothing under 18 pt, under 300 words.

Teal for headings and the highlighted series; terracotta for the key number, set as a stat tile. The central figure is a 3D scatter with its 2D twin beside it.

Built with beamerposter and vellum-colors.sty. The figure is drawn by vellum.py at poster size, scaled by 2.6.

Talk · 16:9 · beamer, dark twin

Talk

The frame title states the claim and the figure's own title block is dropped. Dark twin for a projector in a dark room; light twin for daylight or a deck read later, chosen once per deck.

A four-series figure is revealed one series per slide. A surface is shown at two or three fixed views, followed by its flat twin. Lists of four items at most, no text under 16 pt.

Sizes on a 16:9 frame

Title 28 pt Palatino; body 20 pt Lato, four lines at most; figure text at 2.2 × print size; footer 10 pt with short title, slide number and stamp. Margins 8 mm. Figures drawn at V.size("slide"), 10 × 5.2 in, with vellum.use("dark", scale=2.2).

illustrative simulation on the analytic landscape w(z1, z2) of examples/python/_common.py; not data · Illustrative simulation, labelled on every slide. Figures by examples/python/talk/make_talk_figures.py, built by examples/latex/talk/build.py. The page is the 160 x 90 mm frame enlarged by 1.764 so the 10 in slide figure and the 28/20/10 pt type sizes hold at 1:1.

Pitch deck · ten slides · HTML

Pitch deck

Ten slides for a funder or committee, written to be read without a speaker. Light mode, plain chart forms, key numbers as stat tiles.

Teal for structure, terracotta once per slide for the key point, other colours only inside charts. Each slide title is a claim; a line under each figure gives the number's origin, and the footer the source and date.

Slide order

  1. Claim.
  2. Problem, one stat tile.
  3. Existing work.
  4. Approach, up to five boxes.
  5. Evidence, one highlighted series.
  6. Evidence, small multiples.
  7. Outcomes, three stat tiles.
  8. Milestone timeline.
  9. The ask, in terracotta.
  10. Contact.
UN World Population Prospects (2024), Human Mortality Database (2024), Zijdeman and Ribeira da Silva (2015), Riley (2005); processed by Our World in Data, downloaded 2026-10-12; CC BY 4.0 · Illustrative proposal: the pipeline, plan, budget and ask are invented; the evidence slides use real data.

Report · A4 · HTML and matplotlib

Report

Sections follow the argument: situation, trend, gap, options, ask. Each has one figure whose title is its claim, and the summary repeats those titles. Projections are tint bands with the line dotted after the last observed year; every figure has a dated source.

A4, 20 mm margins, twelve columns with 4 mm gutters, body 10.5 pt, Palatino headings. Key messages in cream boxes with a 2 pt teal left rule. Country comparisons are dot plots sorted by value, focal countries in terracotta.

Host brands. The host keeps its logo, cover and heading colour; the figures follow Vellum. The host's primary colour can take slot one if it is ΔE 15 from terracotta and 3:1 on white.

The seventeen SDG colours fail the distance checks against each other, so they appear only as small icons beside goal names.

Period life expectancy at birth, UN World Population Prospects (2024) and others, processed by Our World in Data, 1950–2023; country outlines and 2019 population estimates, Natural Earth v5.1.2 · Illustrative layout, real data. Every number is computed by the script and written to media/report/numbers.json.

Infographic · 1080 × 1920 px · HTML and SVG

Infographic

A one-page explainer: three to five stat tiles and one figure on white with cream tiles and a Palatino headline. Four text sizes only: number, headline, sentence, source. Tints may be used as large blocks here.

At 1080 px: 64 px margin, twelve columns with 24 px gutters, 8 px base unit, at least 30 % empty space, at most 80 words. Bars start at zero and every comparison states its base. No gradients, photographs behind text or 3D.

One master, several formats

The portrait story (1080 × 1920) is a stack of cards; the square is its first three. One script renders every format and prints every number from the data.
