Media

The medium changes the sizes and the density, never the palette

A journal column, a thesis, an A0 poster, a talk in a dark hall, a pitch deck read without a speaker, an A4 report and a phone-sized infographic. Each medium has its own sizes, density and reader, and each one keeps the same palette, fonts and anatomy. Three of the examples below tell one story, life expectancy since 1950, in three formats.

Papers · 89 or 183 mm · matplotlib

Papers: the title moves to the caption

A paper figure is Vellum with the title block removed. Everything else stays, which is how a paper is still recognisable as yours. The claim becomes the first sentence of the caption; the subtitle's content becomes the caption's next sentences; panel letters stay in the figure and panel claims become the caption's lettered sentences. The footer's content goes in the data and code availability statement, and the file name carries the stamp.

Single column 3.5 in (89 mm), double 7.2 in (183 mm), drawn at the final width and never scaled into a column. Lines 1.2 pt instead of 1.6, markers 20 pt². Four series per panel at most. The figure must survive greyscale printing, which the alternating lightness of the tones guarantees. A journal's own requirements take precedence: if it names a font, use it and keep every other rule.

Files for a journal

Vector PDF with the fonts embedded (pdf.fonttype 42), one file per figure, named fig2_claim_<sha>.pdf. TIFF or EPS converted from the PDF at 600 dpi for line art, never redrawn. Composite figures are composed in the plotting script, not in a drawing program, so they regenerate. A heat map takes one family or lagoon with a legend bar; a 3D figure is allowed once, with its 2D twin as panel b.

Dissertation · 125 mm text width

Dissertation: the whole style, without exception

A thesis is read from the figures first. Every figure is a card a reader can take out of the chapter and understand alone, so unlike a paper the thesis figure keeps its claim title and subtitle inside the figure, and the caption below repeats the claim in one sentence and adds how to read it.

Body in TeX Gyre Pagella 11 pt on a 125 mm text width; Lato for captions, tables, margin notes and figure text; chapter numbers in the teal tone; links in the indigo tone without boxes. Figures are drawn 4.92 in wide. Tables in Lato with lining figures, no vertical rules, a 0.5 pt ink rule above and below the header and at the foot, alternate rows in the card colour.

One language across chapters. The semantic slots hold for the whole thesis: the model is teal, data and the highlighted thing terracotta, bounds ochre and dotted. A reader who learned the colours in chapter 2 reads chapter 5 without a legend. One gradient per variable for the whole thesis.

Before the final build, print one chapter in greyscale on the department printer. A figure that fails gets a label, not a louder colour.

Conference poster · A0 portrait · beamerposter

Posters: one figure with a title, not a paper on a wall

A0 portrait, read from two metres, one claim. A white sheet with cream column cards, one large central figure, three short columns of text, and a Palatino headline at 90 pt that states the claim. Body 28 pt, figure body 23 pt, nothing under 18 pt, under 300 words in all.

One accent, the teal tone, for headings and the highlighted series; terracotta for the single most important number, set as a stat tile. A poster that uses all six families outside its figure looks like a flyer. It may carry the one unusual form Vellum allows per piece, here a 3D scatter with its 2D twin beside it, because a reader standing in front of it has time to learn it.

Built with beamerposter and vellum-colors.sty; the central figure is drawn by vellum.py at the poster size, the one medium where a figure is scaled, by exactly 2.6.

Talk · 16:9 · beamer, dark twin

Talks: a figure with a sentence

A slide is a figure with a sentence. The sentence is the frame title and it is the claim; the figure's own title block is dropped. The dark twin for a projector in a dark hall, where it hides the projector's weakness and the light tones glow; the light twin for a meeting room with daylight or a deck that will be read later. Decide once per deck.

A figure with four series is shown in four steps, each adding a series in its slot colour while the earlier ones stay. A surface is shown at two or three fixed views on consecutive slides, never spinning, and its flat twin follows. No bullet list longer than four, no logo on every slide, no text under 16 pt.

Sizes on a 16:9 frame

Title 28 pt Palatino, body 20 pt Lato at most four lines, figure text at the print scale times 2.2, footer 10 pt muted with the short title, slide number and stamp. Margins 8 mm. Figures drawn at V.size("slide"), 10 × 5.2 in, with vellum.use("dark", scale=2.2).

illustrative simulation on the analytic landscape w(z1, z2) of examples/python/_common.py; not data · Illustrative simulation, labelled on every slide. Figures by examples/python/talk/make_talk_figures.py, built by examples/latex/talk/build.py. The page is the 160 x 90 mm frame enlarged by 1.764 so the 10 in slide figure and the 28/20/10 pt type sizes hold at 1:1.

Pitch deck · ten slides · HTML

Pitch decks: larger numbers, fewer figures

A deck for a funder, a partner or a committee: ten to fifteen slides, read without a speaker as often as with one. Light mode. The reader is not an expert, so every figure is a plain form, every number is a stat tile, and the terracotta highlight is used once per slide for the thing to remember.

Teal for structure, terracotta for the one thing that matters, everything else in ink, secondary, muted and card. A deck that uses mauve or ochre outside a chart has lost its discipline. Every slide stands alone: its title is the claim, a line under the figure says where the number comes from, and the footer carries the source and date.

The arc, in ten slides

  1. The one-sentence claim.
  2. The problem, as one stat tile.
  3. What exists and why it is not enough.
  4. The approach, as at most five boxes.
  5. Evidence: one figure, one highlighted series.
  6. Evidence: small multiples.
  7. What it delivers: three stat tiles.
  8. The plan: a timeline of milestones.
  9. The ask: one number in terracotta.
  10. Contact and the stamp.

Report · A4 · HTML and matplotlib

Reports: an argument with figures at its joints

Define the order of the argument before drawing anything: the situation, the trend, the gap, the options, the ask. Each section has one figure that carries its claim; the executive summary repeats the claims as the figure titles. Every title is a claim a minister could repeat. Units in words, years as years, projections as tint bands with the line dotted after the last observed year, sources under every figure with the year of the data.

A4, 20 mm margins, a twelve-column grid with 4 mm gutters, body 10.5 pt, headings in Palatino. Key messages in cream boxes with a 2 pt teal rule on the left and no other border. Plain forms throughout and one memorable form per report. Country comparisons are dot plots sorted by value with the focal countries in terracotta, never a bar chart of forty bars in six colours.

Living beside a host brand. The host's logo, cover and heading colour are the host's. Inside the figures, Vellum's rules stand. The host's primary colour may take slot one if it passes the same checks (ΔE 15 from terracotta, 3:1 on white); otherwise it stays outside the figures.

The seventeen SDG colours are a brand, not a palette: they fail every distance check beside each other. Use them only as a small icon beside a goal's name.

Period life expectancy at birth, UN World Population Prospects (2024) and others, processed by Our World in Data, 1950–2023; country outlines and 2019 population estimates, Natural Earth v5.1.2 · Illustrative layout, real data. Every number is computed by the script and written to media/report/numbers.json.

Infographic · 1080 × 1920 px · HTML and SVG

Infographics: the number first, then the headline

A one-page explainer read in under a minute by someone who did not ask for it. Three to five stat tiles and one figure on a white sheet with cream tiles and a Palatino headline. The reader meets the number, then the headline, then the sentence, then the source, and there are no other text sizes. It is the one medium where tints may be used as large blocks.

At 1080 px: a 64 px margin, twelve columns with 24 px gutters, an 8 px base unit, at least 30 per cent empty, at most 80 words. Areas proportional to values, bars from zero, the base of every comparison in its label. Two families and one terracotta highlight outside the figure. Never a gradient, never a photograph behind text, never 3D.

One master, several formats

Design the portrait story (1080 × 1920) first, as a stack of cards; the square is its first three cards. Every format is rendered from the same script with its own size, and every number is printed by the script from the data, not typed. A change in the data regenerates every format.
