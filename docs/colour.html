Colour

Muted on purpose, separated by lightness

Every colour in Vellum is a coordinate in a perceptual colour space, chosen by a rule and checked by a computation. This page walks through the nine steps that produced the palette. Anyone who needs a seventh hue or a different surface can derive it by the same steps and run the same checks.

The palette

Read the job first, then the value. A colour used for the wrong job is as wrong as a colour that is not in the list. Click any swatch to copy its hex; the values follow the theme, so switch to the dark twin with the button at the top to see its palette.

Step 1. Work in a perceptual space

A hex code is a coordinate in sRGB, a space made for monitors, not for eyes. Two colours of equal RGB "brightness" can look very different in lightness, and rotating the hue in HSL changes the apparent lightness: yellow looks lighter than blue at the same HSL lightness.

OKLCH (Björn Ottosson, 2020) is built so that equal steps look equal. It has three coordinates: L, lightness, from 0 for black to 1 for white; C, chroma, from 0 for grey upward; and H, the hue angle in degrees. Vellum defines every colour as (L, C, H) and converts to hex only at the end. Colour distances are measured as ΔE, the straight-line distance in OKLab times 100.

The conversion, the colour-vision simulation and the contrast formula are in generators/colorlab.py, about a hundred lines of NumPy; the web version is in vellum-web.js and gives the same numbers.

Why not HSL

HSL and HSV are rotations of the RGB cube. Equal steps in them are not equal to the eye. A palette built in HSL at one "lightness" has a yellow that shouts and a blue that disappears; a gradient interpolated in HSL bends through colours nobody chose.

Step 2. Three steps of one hue

In the painter's vocabulary a tint is a hue plus white, a shade a hue plus black, and a tone a hue plus grey. Vellum keeps the hue angle fixed and moves L and C, which is the same idea done exactly.

  • Tint, for area: bands, fills, the background of a cell. Large areas must be quiet, so chroma is low and lightness near the surface.
  • Tone, for line: curves, dots, bars. Thin marks need contrast against the surface and a visible hue.
  • Deep, for emphasis and for text set on a tint, where it reaches 6:1.

A tint on white is at about 1.6:1 contrast. It can never carry a line or a label. This is the rule that makes a pastel palette usable at all.

Step 3. Six hues, about sixty degrees apart

Numbers are the slot order. The dotted chords join the complement pairs. The dots take the current twin's tones.

Teal at 200°, terracotta at 40°, indigo at 265°, ochre at 85°, mauve at 335° and moss at 140°: a hexadic spread in which every hue has its complement in the set. Teal and terracotta, indigo and ochre, mauve and moss sit roughly opposite each other. The complement pairs are what make the diverging scale and the one-warm-highlight rule possible without a new colour.

At chroma 0.085 the six hues land on the colours of earth pigments: a verdigris teal, a terracotta, an indigo, a yellow ochre, a dusty mauve, a moss green. Pigments are what the eye expects on paper, which is why the palette reads as print rather than screen. The names are kept because they are easier to remember than angles.

The slot order is not the wheel order. It alternates cool and warm, and dark and light, so that neighbouring series differ in hue, in warmth and in lightness at once.

Step 4. Muted colours need lightness to differ

Two colours at equal L and C, sixty degrees apart in hue, differ by 2C·sin(30°) = C in OKLab. At C = 0.085 that is a distance of 8.5, far below the floor of 15 that full-colour readers need to tell neighbouring series apart. A muted palette at one lightness is a set of greys with a faint tint, and hue alone cannot separate them.

Saturating the colours would break the brief. The way out is to alternate lightness: dark tone, light tone, dark tone. Each neighbouring pair then differs in hue and lightness. The tone lightness of each slot was searched by computer, within 0.46–0.64 on the white page and 0.68–0.86 on charcoal, to maximise the smallest distance between neighbours. The search is build_palettes.py; it is seeded, and rerunning it reproduces palettes.json exactly.

Step 5. Colour-vision deficiency

About eight per cent of men see red and green weakly. Red–green pairs collapse for them. The simulation used here is Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) at severity 1.0, applied in linear RGB, with distances in OKLab. The target is a distance of 8 or more between neighbouring series, with 6 as a floor that is legal only with a second encoding such as labels.

The alternating order keeps a cool hue between every two warm ones, and lightness contrast survives any deficiency, so the palette passes at 11.4 in the light twin and 11.3 in the dark one.

Never let colour be the only carrier of identity. End labels, and a legend past four series, are the second channel. They are mandatory, not decoration.

Step 6. Contrast against the surface

Marks need 3:1 against the surface (WCAG 2.1, non-text contrast). Body text needs 4.5:1, large text 3:1. Contrast is computed from relative luminance with the WCAG formula.

Muted text, at 3.67:1 on white, is reserved for the unit line, the ticks and the footer: small, secondary text a reader scans rather than reads. Anything that must be read at body size is in ink or secondary.

The tones were searched against the cream card, the harder case, where the lightest tone reaches 3.03:1; on the white page every tone clears 3.27:1.

Step 7. One hue for magnitude, two complements for polarity

A sequential scale is one hue family in seven steps from near the surface to past the deep step, lightness falling in equal steps and chroma rising, then falling, so the middle steps are the most coloured. Use it for magnitude only; prepend the surface as step zero when the lowest value should vanish. The default is teal; a second map on the same page takes terracotta. Never two ramps in one panel.

A diverging scale is two complements, terracotta and teal, through a neutral midpoint that is a grey, so that "nothing" reads as nothing. Warm is below the baseline and cool above. The midpoint is fixed at the baseline value and never centred by the data range.

A rainbow is never used: it has no order the eye can read, and its lightness rises and falls along the scale.

Step 8. Status is a separate set

Good, warning, serious and critical mean something before the reader looks at a legend. They are saturated on purpose, used only on small marks with an icon and a word, and never as a series colour. They sit at a distance of at least 7.4 from every tone in the light twin, so a status mark cannot be mistaken for series four.

Warning, at 2.0:1 on white, must carry its label for that reason.

Step 9. Gradients

In Vellum a gradient is a data scale or it is absent. Three named multi-hue gradients extend the single-family ramps when a scale needs more range: lagoon (moss, teal, indigo) for general magnitude and model outputs, ember (ochre, terracotta, mauve) for a warm magnitude such as a risk or a rate of failure, and shore (ochre, moss, teal) for a second map when lagoon is already on the page.

Lightness carries the order

Nine stops, OKLCH lightness from 0.94 to 0.36 in equal steps of about 0.07. A reader orders two colours first by lightness, so a gradient with monotone lightness is ordered for everyone, in greyscale and under colour-vision deficiency. The generator asserts this before it writes a file.

Chroma follows a bell

Chroma is 0.02 at both ends and 0.085 in the middle (a sine to the power 0.8). The pale end stays a tint and the dark end a shade, neither looks neon, and the peak equals the chroma of the tones, so a gradient has the same muted register as the series colours.

Hue travels at most 125°

Analogous hues blend without passing through grey. A longer path reaches the complement and crosses a colour the reader has no name for. All three gradients stay in the warm half or the cool half; none runs through red against green.

Two complements make a diverging scale, never a magnitude scale

Join the teal tone #419BA0 to the terracotta tone #A1644F, both at chroma 0.085. Interpolated in RGB, the middle is #718078 at chroma 0.021; in OKLab it is #7F8278 at chroma 0.015. A quarter or less of the chroma of the ends, in either space. Opposite hues cancel to grey: that is a property of the wheel, not of the method of interpolation.

The diverging scale uses this on purpose, with a designed neutral #E1DDD8 on the baseline. A complementary gradient used for magnitude shows a grey band in the middle that reads as "no data" or "zero", which is false for a magnitude.

The four allowed uses of a gradient

  1. A continuous scale: a heat map, a surface, a choropleth, a scatter coloured by a third variable.
  2. The legend bar of that scale.
  3. An open interval: the end of a projection, as a fade to the surface over the stretch the claim does not cover.
  4. An ordered sequence along a line: a trajectory whose colour is time.

Cells, gaps and missing values

  1. A gradient is applied across cells, never inside one cell. Cells are separated by 1.2 pt gaps in the surface colour.
  2. A cell with no value is drawn in the card colour with a hatch in the terracotta tone. Never the lowest stop and never white, because both read as a real low value.
  3. A number inside a cell is in the surface colour on stops 7 to 9 and in ink on stops 1 to 5, which both reach 4.5:1. Stop 6 of lagoon and shore reaches 4.5:1 with neither, so a number there takes a halo or is left out.
  4. A legend bar is 6 to 8 px thick with no outline, three to five ticks at round values, and the ends labelled with values, not with "low" and "high".
  5. One gradient per variable for the whole document. If a quantity is lagoon in chapter 2, it is lagoon in chapter 5.

Semantic slots

When a set of figures has natural entities, the family follows the entity across every figure, so a reader who learns "the model is teal" keeps it for the whole document. When a figure has no such entities, a parameter sweep for example, the slot order applies and nothing else.

And when the figure is about one thing, that thing is terracotta and everything else is rule grey.
