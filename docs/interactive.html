Interactive

The static figure first, interaction as a layer

Each figure on this page reads completely before you touch it: claim, labels and values are all there at first paint. Interaction adds a way in: a tooltip that leads with the value, a crosshair that finds the year, a highlight that greys the rest, a surface you can turn. Every one follows the theme, so switch to the dark twin and watch them redraw.

A surface you can turn, read from its flat twin

Turn the surface to see its shape: two peaks of unequal height and the saddle between them, the feature the title names. Then read values on the flat twin, where pointing at any spot marks the same point on the surface, as an open ring when a ridge hides it. The view starts at the house default, orthographic at elevation 24° and azimuth −58°, and returns there with "Reset view". Flat shading by height on the lagoon gradient, no lighting and no mesh lines, transparent panes with three ticks per axis, contours on the floor: the surface shows the shape, the twin is where values are read.

Choose the countries; the colours stay put

Add and remove countries, up to four, and watch the colours. A country keeps its colour for as long as it stays chosen, so removing one never repaints the others: colour follows the entity, never its rank. Lines end in ringed dots and direct labels instead of a legend, the world sits behind in rule grey as context, and the crosshair reads every line in a year at once. The claim is recomputed for the countries you choose.

A progressive reveal, as on a slide

This is how Vellum shows a figure in a talk. Each step adds one series in its slot colour, with its end label, while the earlier ones stay, and the axes and label positions never move. The last step puts the group the claim is about in terracotta and greys the rest. Press 1 and step through it as an audience would see it.

A heat map on a gradient, with missing cells hatched

Each cell is one type of disaster in one decade, coloured on a log scale on the lagoon gradient, so both millions and single digits stay visible. Point at or tab through the cells to read them; the legend bar marks where each sits on the scale. Hatched cells mean no record, not zero: Vellum never draws missing data as the lowest colour of the scale. Sort by total to see which disasters dominated, and read the top rows from left to right to see how far they fell.

A scatter with the axes in your hands

Choose any two of the four measures. Each species keeps its colour and its own fit line, labelled directly with its correlation, and the claim is recomputed for the axes you pick. With beak length against beak depth, every species shows a positive relation while the pooled birds show a negative one: Simpson's paradox, stated in the title and drawn as a dotted grey line.

A world map in five stated classes

Five classes of the teal ramp at the 2023 quintiles, held fixed for every year, so a colour means the same in 1950 as in 2023. Slide back to 1950 and almost the whole map drops into the lowest class. Small countries vanish on a world map, so each also appears as a dot in the strip below, linked to the map. One ramp, stated classes, land without a value in the card colour with a terracotta hatch, borders in the grid colour, no labels.

A network where one node is the point

Characters are linked when they appear in the same chapter, in a force layout that is seeded and run to rest before the page paints, so it never moves. The six largest groups take the six tones in slot order and the rest are rule grey. Hover, tap or search a character: it and its neighbours turn terracotta and everything else fades to the grid colour. The "highlight one, grey the rest" rule turns a hairball into a readable neighbourhood.

Colour tools

The three tools from the colour page, gathered here. Each computes its numbers live from vellum.json, with the same formulas as the Python generators. The simulator recomputes every series colour exactly as a reader with each kind of colour-vision deficiency sees it, against a common saturated default palette: under protanopia two of the default's colours fall to 0.7 apart. The gradient inspector shows why a rainbow is never used: its lightness climbs and then falls, so it has no order the eye can read.

How these are built. Plain JavaScript modules on vellum-web.js, which reads the same vellum.json as every other tool. No framework and no chart library: the anatomy (hairline grid, unit line, ringed dots, end labels with leaders, value-first tooltips) is a dozen small functions. Each figure has a table view under it for readers who do not use a pointer.
