Once per project

Put the style beside your scripts

Download the style files and unzip them into your project. There is no package to install beyond your plotting library: each file is loaded from the folder it sits in.

Fonts: Palatino Linotype and Corbel on Windows; TeX Gyre Pagella and Lato elsewhere.

Write the claim first

The claim is the one sentence a reader should leave with. Write it before you plot, at the top of the script, where it becomes the title.

Carbon dioxide at Mauna Loawhat rosea verb in every year from 1959 to 2019,where and when by 96 ppm in allhow much

  1. One sentence with a verb; a number where there is one.
  2. Sentence case, no full stop.
  3. No sentence comes? Explore with plain plots until one does.

Cover the title. A colleague should name the claim within three seconds; if not, remove a series or split a panel.

Let the reader pick the medium

Ask where the figure will be read. The answer fixes its size, its scale and where the claim goes. Draw at that size; never scale a figure afterwards.

read insizescalethe claim goes
a paperV.size("single") or "double"1.0; lines 1.2 ptinto the caption; no title block
a dissertation4.92 in wide, the 125 mm text1.0in the figure, so it reads outside the chapter
a talkV.size("slide")2.2; the dark twin in a dark roominto the frame title
a posterV.size("poster")2.6into the headline, read from two metres
a report or a web pageV.size("double")1.0in the figure
 
# a talk: every size × 2.2
V = vellum.use("light", scale=2.2)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=V.size("slide"))
# a talk in ggplot2
theme_vellum(base_size = 9 * 2.2)

Every figure

Start from the nearest example

You never start from an empty file, and you never need most of the repository. Four layers, and what to do with each:

Use as it is

style/

Colours, type, sizes and the helpers that draw the anatomy. Load it; never edit it.

Copy and trim

examples/

Take the script nearest your form and keep its draw(V) function. Leave out _common, META and save(): they file figures into this site.

Write yourself

your script

The data, the claim, the form, the ticks and limits, and which series get a label.

Ignore

generators/ tools/ docs/

They rebuild Vellum and this site. A figure never needs them.

7
Every figure · after the last change

Check, then save both twins

  1. Render it and look at it, at its printed size.
  2. Run the twenty checks: claim, colour, anatomy, file.
  3. Save a PDF with embedded fonts for print and a PNG at 300 dpi for screens, from the same script.
  4. Draw it again with vellum.use("dark") for a dark hall.
  5. Keep the script beside the figure; the footer names its commit.

The script is the figure. Change the data or the claim, run it again, and both twins follow.
