0. Is it a figure?

One number with a comparison goes in the text or on a stat tile, not in a one-bar chart. A two-slice pie is a sentence. Use a table when the reader will look values up.

73.2years of life expected at birth, worldwide, 2023against 46.4 in 1950
−1.7years lost between 2019 and 2021recovered by 2023

1. Change over time

Line chart

The default. Tones in slot order, 1.6 pt, ringed end dots, end labels; up to four series without a legend. Uncertainty: a tint band at 0.55 opacity, with a note. Member runs: 0.7 pt in the tone, mean on top. Time on x. Counts start at zero; otherwise the unit line gives the axis start. Log axes get a note.

Area and stacked area

Tints, 1.2 pt surface gaps, slot order from the bottom, each layer labelled at its right end in its deep step. Stack only parts of a whole. Streamgraphs on posters and slides only, noting the centred baseline.

Slope and bump charts

Slope chart for two moments: tones for the claimed series, rule grey for the rest, labels at both ends. Bump chart for ranks: one series terracotta, the rest rule grey.

Second y axis

Allowed for two series on a shared x axis when the claim is about their timing or co-movement and an index to a common base would hide the units.

  • The series take slots 1 and 2.
  • Tick labels stay in muted text.
  • Each axis has its unit line at its top, left-aligned on the left and right-aligned on the right, led by a short line key in the series colour.
  • One grid only: the right axis's ticks fall on the left axis's gridlines.
  • End labels name the series.

2. Comparison and ranking

Bar chart

Horizontal for word labels, vertical for ordered categories. One series, one colour: teal tint, with the highlighted bar in the terracotta tone. Values at bar ends in ink when they matter. Sort by value unless the order means something. Error bars in secondary, no caps. No value-coloured nominal bars, no one-bar charts. Several variables per item: a table or small multiples.

Dot plot, lollipop and dumbbell

Beyond about twelve categories: ringed dots on 0.7 pt rule-grey stems. Before and after: two dots joined by a stem (dumbbell).

3. Distribution

Histogram and density

Histogram: one series in the tint, 1.2 pt gaps. Several distributions: density curves in the tones over tint at 0.3 opacity, or a ridgeline in slot order from the top. The subtitle gives the bin width or bandwidth.

Box, violin, beeswarm

Under about thirty points: a beeswarm of ringed dots. Up to a few hundred: a box plot, tone outline, tint fill, 0.7 pt whiskers. More: a violin in the tint with a tone outline. The unit line defines box and whiskers.

4. Relation

Scatter plot

Ringed dots. Up to three families unlabelled (the first three tones are distinct in every pair); more need direct labels. Predicted against observed: a rule-grey "y = x" line. Fit line 1.6 pt in the tone, band in the tint. Label only points the claim names.

Heat map and 2D density

One ramp, teal by default; lagoon when values span many decades or low cells must stay visible. 1.2 pt surface lines between cells, no spines, legend bar without outline. Values in cells only where they help. Missing or failed cells hatched in the terracotta tone on the card colour. Ticks are parameter values, not indices.

Connected scatter and correlogram

Connected scatter: 1.6 pt line, ringed dots, years labelled every few steps. Correlogram: half matrix on the diverging scale, midpoint at zero.

5. Part of a whole

Default: a horizontal stacked bar of tints with surface gaps, labels in the deep step inside segments. A ten-by-ten waffle for a share to count. Treemaps only for hierarchies; Voronoi treemaps for posters and reports, not papers. Pies and doughnuts: six segments at most, shares written on, not for close values.

Word cloud

Allowed as an overview of counts, not for precise comparison.

  • Words horizontal.
  • Font size ∝ √count, so a word's area tracks its count.
  • Deterministic layout: the same data give the same picture.
  • Colour only to mark a role or group.
  • The exact counts of the top few words given beside it.

6. Position: maps

Land in the card colour or the classes, borders 0.4 pt in the grid colour, water as the surface. Choropleth: one ramp, classes (quantiles or equal steps) in the legend, no-data countries hatched. Bubble map: ringed dots sized by area, three reference sizes in the legend. No tiles, relief or labels beyond places the claim names. Maps are flat.

Small countries vanish on a world map; add a dot plot of the same values.

7. Flow, trees and networks

Phylogeny and tree

Branches 1.0 pt in secondary, tips as ringed dots. Claimed clades in the tones (teal, then terracotta), the rest in secondary. Time axis at the bottom in muted, present at the right. No node circles; support values only if the claim is about them.

Network

Edges 0.4 pt rule grey at 0.6 opacity; nodes as ringed dots coloured by community, up to six. Highlight: node and neighbours in terracotta, the rest in the grid colour. The subtitle names the layout method.

Sankey and chord

Flows in tints at 0.6 opacity, nodes as bars in the tones, labels in ink. Chord diagrams with more than about eight groups: small multiples.

The third axis

Use a 3D axis only when three measured quantities form one shape and the shape is the claim. Otherwise the third dimension is a colour, size, panel or contour. Four questions, in order:

  1. Are x, y and z of equal standing, z = f(x, y)? A category, a count per group or a time is not a third axis.
  2. Is the shape the claim: a ridge, trough, saddle, peak location, several optima?
  3. Can the task be done from a heat map with contours? If yes, draw that.
  4. If yes to 1 and 2 and no to 3, draw the 3D figure and its 2D twin.
settingvalue, and why
projectionorthographic: perspective makes the near edge look larger, which is false for data
viewelevation 24°, azimuth −58°: shows the shape and the floor, neither floor axis foreshortened
box aspect1 : 1 : 0.62
panestransparent, hairline edges and grid in the grid colour, three ticks per axis
colourrepeats z with a named gradient; flat shading, no light source, because a lit surface changes colour with its slope
meshnone; faces about 1 % of the width (90 × 90); a wireframe only when the shape must be seen through
floor6 % below the minimum, with seven contour lines in the rule colour
filethe surface rasterised at 200 dpi or more, the text vector

The 2D twin

A heat map with contours of the same data, gradient, limits and axes, as panel b or in the supplement. Values are read from the twin; the shape is seen in 3D. On the web a rotatable version may sit beside the static one: try it.

No 3D for

Bars, pies, areas, extruded or ribbon lines, perspective tilts on flat charts, shadows, bevels, reflections: they add distortion and make front bars look bigger. No 3D at all in pitch decks, reports or infographics.

Across all forms

  1. Identity: tones in slot order. Magnitude: one ramp. Polarity: the diverging pair. State: status colours.
  2. When the claim is about one thing: terracotta for it, rule grey for the rest.
  3. Facet into small multiples before adding a seventh colour. A second y axis only under the rules above.
  4. Each form has direct labels and a note saying what the marks are.
  5. A form the reader may not know gets a subtitle sentence on how to read it.
